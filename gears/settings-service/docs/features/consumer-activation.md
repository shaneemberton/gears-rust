## 1. Feature Context

### 1.1 Overview

Lets every instance of a consuming gear — one process, identified by the `instance_id` its SDK generates — subscribe to the settings it materializes, learn which of them changed at the tenant it serves, re-read them and report what it applied, and keeps, per subscription of each instance, whether the current value is confirmed (ADR-004). Every value change records, inside its own transaction and under the key's mutation lock, one activation state per subscription it expects — every affected instance of a subscriber at a tenant, less the silent ones while a sibling is expected — `awaiting`, with the expected value at the subscription's tenant, the change's audit record id as `change_ref` and no delivery counted; the end of the request publishes one filtered `change_notification` per affected subscriber and tenant on the activation topic of the platform Event Broker, which reaches every instance of the subscriber with an interest at that tenant; a delivery loop on every replica re-publishes what stays `awaiting`, counting the publications the broker accepted, calls an instance silent once `K` of them went unanswered — its subscriber's change then reads `incomplete` — and forgets a subscription silent for a day; and the instance's report, an SDK call, settles its own state against the expectation recorded when the change committed — a late report on a silent state included — and appends an `activation` audit record, as each call of silence does.

### 1.2 Purpose

A consumer that has already materialized a setting — a connection pool sized by it, a socket bound from it, a rendered file — never re-reads it unless something says it changed, and an administrator who set a value cannot tell from the `set` response whether the platform runs on it. This feature carries the protocol of DESIGN-activation under ADR-003, as ADR-004 refines it: a thin event carrying identifiers only, a pull of the current value through the reader, and an outcome reported through the SDK by each consumer instance for itself and verified against a snapshot, the wait for it bounded by a report timeout counted in accepted deliveries.

Two distributions follow a committed change, and this feature owns the second of them (DESIGN-activation §1.1 *Two distinct distributions*):

| | Replica cache invalidation (entry 2.12) | Consumer activation (this feature) |
|---|---|---|
| Who receives | Every Settings Service replica | Only subscribers of the changed key at a tenant whose effective value changed |
| Subscription | None | Per exact key and tenant, per consumer instance, explicit, through the SDK |
| Payload | The tenant's full changed-key set | The subscriber's keys at that tenant with a due `awaiting` state of any of its instances, each key once with its `change_ref` |
| Acknowledged | No | Yes — one activation state per subscription of an instance, the wait bounded by the report timeout counted in accepted deliveries |
| Event | `cache_invalidate`, cache topic | `change_notification`, activation topic |

Seven properties shape the feature.

**One activation state per subscription, one subscription per instance.** A subscription is `(subscriber, instance, key, tenant)`: each process of a consuming gear registers for itself under the `instance_id` its SDK generates, and a gear running one process is the one-instance case. A subscription holds at most one activation state. The latest change that affects it sets what the instance is expected to apply; a later change overwrites the states of every instance of the subscriber at that key and tenant at once and resets them to `awaiting`, and that overwrite is the whole of supersession, so every state of a subscriber at one key and tenant names the same `change_ref`. `change_ref` is the id of the change's audit record, which correlates the change, the notification and the report. The change outcome of a subscriber — `failed` over `incomplete` over `awaiting` over `succeeded` — is derived from its instances' states at read time and never stored.

**The state commits with the value.** The activation state is written in the change's own transaction, under the key's mutation lock held from before the candidate step until the commit and the local eviction, so no committed change can lack its expectation and no registration or retire lands between the subscriptions a change saw and its commit.

**Delivery repeats until reported or called silent, and the wait is bounded in deliveries.** The `awaiting` states are the delivery queue: the end of the request publishes them promptly, and the delivery loop re-publishes each due one every `redeliver_interval_seconds` until its instance reports or a later change replaces it. Each publication the broker accepts counts one delivery of the states it carried while due; a state still `awaiting` after `K = ceil(activation_report_timeout_seconds / redeliver_interval_seconds)` of them is called `silent`, which closes its subscriber's change `incomplete`, neither discarded nor failed: a late report still settles it. Time during which nothing can be delivered counts nothing.

**A silent instance is left out, re-enters, and is forgotten.** While a sibling instance of its subscriber at that key and tenant is expected, a silent instance is not expected by later changes, yet still receives its gear's notifications through its own consumer group; any report or registration of it for the key ends its silence and re-arms its own `silent` state of the current change, and a subscription silent for a day is forgotten, its states with it, the history keeping every report and every call of silence.

**No value in any notification.** A `change_notification` carries the tenant, the subscriber and the keys with their `change_ref`, nothing else — no instance either; the consumer re-reads under its own identity with the freshness option. The report is the deliberate exception: it echoes the applied value, a salted hash for a `secret`-trait setting.

**The outcome is an SDK call.** `report_outcome` reaches the Activation Outcome Recorder directly — not a broker event, not a REST route — carrying the reporting process's `instance_id`, and answers `applied`, `ignored` or `not_subscribed`.

**The broker is a soft dependency.** Without a broker a write commits, its states stay `awaiting`, the delivery loop's publishing idles — no publication is accepted, so no delivery counts and no instance is called silent — while its forgetting and orphan sweep run on every tick, and delivery resumes once the broker resolves.

**Requirements**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-fr-consumer-activation` (the per-instance account, the report timeout, the silent-instance lifecycle), `cpt-cf-settings-service-contract-consumer-activation`, `cpt-cf-settings-service-fr-live-read-activation` (the change signal a consumer self-reacts to), `cpt-cf-settings-service-nfr-reliability-validated-set` (a refused or stale write stores no activation state), `cpt-cf-settings-service-nfr-security-baseline` (identifiers only in the notification stream, a hash in place of a secret in a report), `cpt-cf-settings-service-nfr-ops-set-monitoring` (the activation and mutation-lock metrics)

## 2. Actor Flows (CDSL)

The flows name their actor by exception where no human acts: the record flow is triggered by an administrator's write — named as the platform administrator, a tenant administrator's write within its subtree taking the same path — and the publish, deliver and lifecycle flows by the gear itself, named after the Event Broker, the system actor the PRD gives the signal. The subscribe, react, report and retire flows are the consuming gear's, through the SDK, each run by one consumer instance for itself. The lifecycle flow is the delivery loop's one control loop and owns its tick; the deliver flow is the body of one pass of it, whose forgetting and orphan sweep run on every tick and whose call of silence and publishing run only on a tick with a broker.

**Use cases**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-usecase-review-and-set`, `cpt-cf-settings-service-usecase-resolve-effective-value`

Throughout, a tenant is a bare lowercase UUID string, and platform scope is the root tenant's id, an ordinary tenant id and never an absent one. "The activation topic" is `gts.cf.core.events.topic.v1~cf.core.settings.activation.v1`, "the `change_notification` type" is `gts.cf.core.events.event.v1~cf.core.settings.change_notification.v1~`, and "the activation subject type" is `gts.cf.core.settings.activation.v1~`. "The subscriber" is the identity of the calling gear — the subject id of the security context the SDK `Context` carries, as text, never a namespace or module name — never an argument the caller passes; the identity is trusted, not verified, and a call whose subject is of an interactive subject type is refused. A subscription handle is live from the moment `subscribe` returns it until the caller drops it or `unsubscribe()` releases its last key. "The instance" is the calling process: its `instance_id` is a UUIDv7 the SDK generates once per process at first use, shared by every subscribing gear in the process, and its `instance_label` the host name the SDK reads once at first use, at most 128 characters, absent when it cannot be read; the SDK client carries the id on every registration, report and unsubscribe, and the label on every registration, never as an argument the caller passes, and a restarted process is a new instance. A subscription and a state are the instance's own: `(subscriber, instance_id, key, tenant)` identifies each. "Expected" names a subscription a change wrote a state for; "silent" names a state the delivery loop called silent and a subscription whose `silent_since` is set. `K` is `ceil(activation_report_timeout_seconds / redeliver_interval_seconds)`, `20` at the defaults, the accepted deliveries after which a due `awaiting` state is called silent. A state is due when it is `awaiting` and its `last_notified_at` is absent or at least `redeliver_interval_seconds` old. `SILENT_FORGET_SECONDS` is `86400`, one day, and `MAX_INSTANCES_PER_SUBSCRIPTION` is `64`, both constants, not configuration. Every writer that changes both an instance's state and its subscription in one transaction changes the state first and finishes one instance before it touches another — a report and a registration clear `silent_since` last, the call of silence sets it after its state update, and an unsubscribe, an eviction and the forgetting delete the states before the subscription (DESIGN-activation §4.2 *Statement order*). Every wait with backoff on the broker or on a registration — the SDK's creation of its consumer group, its join, and its retries of a `pending` key — is exponential from 1 s to a 30 s cap with jitter, as in entry 2.12, and is not configurable; the delivery loop retries its broker lookup and the activation types' registration on its own tick (`inst-ca-lifecycle-7`), and the key's mutation lock waits on a schedule of its own (`inst-ca-lock-3`).

### Subscribe to Settings

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-subscribe`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-internal-caller`

**Success Scenarios**:
- `subscribe` returns its handle at once; each key the gear may read becomes `registered` with the fresh effective value at the tenant, read after its registration committed, under the process's own subscription — one per instance, key and tenant — and the gear's process holds one anonymous consumer group on the activation topic with an interest for that tenant
- A repeat registration of the same instance keeps its subscription, refreshes its `instance_label` and ends its silence: the subscription's `silent_since` is cleared and the instance's `silent` state of the current change, if any, is re-armed to `awaiting` and notified again; an instance the current change left out stays out of it and is expected from the next
- An instance registering after a change has no state of it: it materializes from its fresh read and is expected from the next change
- A registration past `MAX_INSTANCES_PER_SUBSCRIPTION` registrations of the subscriber for the key at the tenant evicts the oldest silent or never-asked one first, and commits
- A key that cannot be registered yet — not declared until a later boot of its owner — stays `pending`, is retried, and on its registration hands the handler its fresh value in a bundle without `change_ref`, which owes no report
- A key whose report was answered `not_subscribed` — the instance forgotten, evicted, or deleted out from under the process — is `pending` with reason `NotRegistered` and registered again while a live handle holds it; a notification carrying it meanwhile is still applied, as a bundle without `change_ref` that owes no report
- A retired key is registered and reported `registered` with its value absent; its next change after a reactivation writes the subscription's state

**Error Scenarios**:
- Empty `keys` or a malformed tenant: an error, no handle, nothing registered
- A registration reaching the service without an `instance_id` — possible only past the SDK client: an invalid-input error, nothing registered
- A 65th tenant interest of the gear in the process: every key `refused`, nothing registered
- The subscriber may not read the key at the tenant (`403`): that key `refused`, terminal
- A key refused `NotFound` before the lock, a lock wait exhausted, the authorization service unavailable, the registration cap reached with no registration to evict (`Unavailable`), a database error before the commit, a fresh read failing after the commit, or a missing broker grant: that key `pending`, retried while the handle is live, a warning each minute

### Record the Activation State with a Change

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-record`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-platform-admin`

**Success Scenarios**:
- Each committed value change writes, in its own transaction, one `awaiting` activation state per subscription it expects, with the expected value at the subscription's tenant, the change's audit record id as `change_ref` and `deliveries` `0`; a subscription it does not affect is untouched
- Of the affected instances of one subscriber at a tenant, the change expects those not silent; a silent one is left out while a sibling is expected — its state of the earlier change deleted, so it is outside this change's account — and when every one is silent the change expects all of them again; both decided by the one statement that writes the states
- A registration, an unsubscribe or a restriction change of the same key waits for the change in flight, or the change waits for it; changes of different keys never wait for each other
- The pairs of subscriber and tenant the request touched are handed to the end-of-request publish

**Error Scenarios**:
- The key's mutation lock is not acquired within the budget: the change answers `Unavailable` — `503` on a single-value route, per item in `SetResult[]` — with nothing stored
- The Tenant Resolver cannot answer a subscription tenant's chain: the write fails closed with nothing stored
- A fallback or staged plaintext fetch fails — its per-setting authorization decision denied or undecidable, the store read or its `secret_use` record failing: the change is refused `503` with nothing of it stored; a `secret_use` record already written stays
- The transaction rolls back — a stale tag, a retired declaration, a failed audit append — or its commit fails: nothing is stored, no activation state is written, and the lock is released

### Publish Notifications at the End of a Request

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-publish`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-event-broker`

**Success Scenarios**:
- A request whose changes affected subscriptions hands the broker one `change_notification` per pair of subscriber and tenant it touched, after its commits and after its `cache_invalidate` broadcasts, each carrying every key with a due `awaiting` state of any instance of that pair, once, with its `change_ref`; the states the request wrote are due
- Once the broker accepts a notification, each due state it bundled has its `last_notified_at` stamped and one delivery counted, and no other state
- The caller's response is the same whatever happened to the notifications

**Error Scenarios**:

### Deliver the Awaiting States

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-deliver`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-event-broker`

**Success Scenarios**:
- On every tick, broker or not, one pass forgets each subscription silent for longer than `SILENT_FORGET_SECONDS`, together with its states, and removes the states whose subscription no longer exists
- On a tick with a broker, the pass calls silent each due `awaiting` state that has had `K` accepted deliveries without a report — its subscriber's change then reads `incomplete`, and one `activation` record with `outcome = silent` names the instance under the change — and re-publishes every other due `awaiting` state whose notification was lost, refused, skipped or not yet answered once `redeliver_interval_seconds` has passed since `last_notified_at`, and at once when an overwrite or a re-arm cleared it, counting one delivery on each due state of every publication the broker accepted
- Exactly one replica scans per tick; the others skip the pass without waiting
- States whose subscription no longer exists are removed by one conditional delete, whatever their status

**Error Scenarios**:
- The scan lock held by another replica: this replica's pass ends at once
- No broker resolvable, the root tenant's id unknown, or the activation types not registered: the pass forgets and sweeps, and calls no instance silent and publishes nothing, so no delivery is counted
- A publish refused or timed out: the rows stay `awaiting`, no delivery is counted, and they are due again at the next interval
- A call of silence that loses a race to a report or a change: its guarded update matches nothing, and it sets no `silent_since` and writes no record
- The broker lost during the pass: the rest of the pass is abandoned and the states are kept
- `stop` during a pass: the pass finishes or is abandoned within the shutdown deadline, and every `awaiting` state is kept

### Report an Outcome

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-report`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-internal-caller`

**Success Scenarios**:
- A report on the instance's own `awaiting` state settles it `succeeded` or `failed`, appends one `activation` audit record naming the instance in the same transaction, and answers `applied`; a late report on its `silent` state is accounted the same way, the state keeping its `silent_since`
- A report of a subscribed instance for no state of the current change, a settled state, a stale `change_ref`, or a state settled or replaced concurrently answers `ignored`, settles nothing and writes no audit record; it still ends the instance's silence for the key, and a stale one re-arms the instance's `silent` state of the current change to `awaiting`; a repeat, decided on a settled state, re-arms nothing
- A report of an instance with no subscription for the key at the tenant — forgotten, evicted, unsubscribed or purged — answers `not_subscribed` and writes nothing; the SDK registers the key again while a live handle holds it
- A report never touches a sibling instance's state, and a report naming another gear's instance touches nothing of that gear

### Unsubscribe a Handle

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-unsubscribe`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-internal-caller`

**Success Scenarios**:
- `unsubscribe()` on a handle releases each of its keys; a key no other live handle of the gear in this process holds has the instance's own subscription deleted together with its activation states, nothing is reported for them, a late report for the key is answered `not_subscribed`, and the subscriber's change outcome is re-derived over its other instances' states
- A key another live handle of the gear in this process still holds leaves this handle only: its subscription and its state stay for that handle. The subscription is the instance's: the subscriptions and states of the gear's other processes are untouched, so a gear that runs several processes is unsubscribed from the key only when its last process unsubscribes
- On the stop of the process's settings-service gear — which comes with the stop of every gear of the process — the SDK unsubscribes every subscription of the process under its `instance_id`, whichever gear's identity it was made under, best-effort within the shutdown deadline, without waiting for the handles to be dropped; the toolkit stops every gear of a process together, so a stop of one gear alone is not a requirement
- An unsubscribe racing a value write of the key waits for the write's commit, then deletes the state the write committed
- When the gear's last live handle in the process is gone — dropped or unsubscribed — the process's consumer group is left and deleted

**Error Scenarios**:
- A key with no subscription of the caller's at that tenant under the `instance_id` the call carries — another subscriber's, or an `instance_id` the caller's subscriber never registered for the key at the tenant: `404` for that key, nothing of anyone deleted; a registered sibling instance's id is accepted and deletes that sibling's subscription, as the trust model allows
- An unsubscribe reaching the service without an `instance_id` — possible only past the SDK client: an invalid-input error, nothing deleted
- The key's lock not acquired within the budget: that key answers `Unavailable`, stays subscribed and keeps its state on the handle and its pending retries; the SDK does not retry the unsubscribe, and the caller may call `unsubscribe()` again
- An unsubscribe of the graceful stop that fails or does not finish by the shutdown deadline: the key stays registered and follows the silent path
- The group cannot be deleted: logged at `warn`, the group stays in the broker as a crashed process's does

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - The consuming gear calls `unsubscribe()` on a handle explicitly; dropping a handle never unsubscribes anything - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - Stop the pending retries only of the keys whose unsubscribe below answers `unsubscribed` or `404`, each as it is answered; a key answered `Unavailable` keeps its state on the handle and its pending retries - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - **FOR EACH** key of the handle, the unsubscribes of the gear's handles in this process taken one at a time → invoke `unsubscribe` with the subscriber from the SDK `Context`, the process's `instance_id`, the key and the handle's tenant, unless the key is left to another handle below - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-3`
   1. [x] - `p1` - **IF** another live handle of the gear in this process holds the key at the handle's tenant → release the key from this handle only, answer `unsubscribed` for it, and continue with the next key: the subscription and its state stay for that handle, and since the unsubscribes are taken one at a time two handles releasing one key cannot each see the other as its holder - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-17`
   2. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the call reaching the service carries no `instance_id` — possible only past the SDK client, which always sets it → answer the typed invalid-input error for the key with nothing deleted and no lock taken, and continue with the next key - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-18`
   3. [x] - `p1` - Take the key's mutation lock (`cpt-cf-settings-service-algo-consumer-activation-lock`) - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-4`
   4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the wait is exhausted → answer `Unavailable` for the key with nothing deleted, the key staying subscribed, and continue with the next key - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-5`
   5. [x] - `p1` - DB: SELECT the subscription of the subscriber, the `instance_id`, the key and the tenant; **IF** none — another subscriber's subscription, or an `instance_id` the caller's subscriber never registered for the key at the tenant → release the lock, answer `404` for the key, and continue with the next key; the `instance_id` is trusted within the subscriber's identity, so a registered sibling instance's id is found and that sibling's subscription is the one deleted below - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-6`
   6. [x] - `p1` - DB: DELETE that subscription's `activation_state` rows, then the subscription, in one transaction; nothing is reported for the deleted rows, and the subscriptions and rows of the subscriber's other instances are untouched - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-7`
   7. [x] - `p1` - Commit the transaction - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-8`
   8. [x] - `p1` - Release the lock and await the release - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-15`
   9. [x] - `p1` - Log the unsubscribe at `info` with the subscriber, the `instance_id`, the key and the tenant - `inst-ca-unsubscribe-16`

4. ### Run the Activation Half of the Broker Lifecycle

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-lifecycle`

**Actor**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-actor-event-broker`

**Success Scenarios**:
- Gear init completes whether or not a broker exists; the delivery loop ticks on every replica, refreshing the activation gauges and running one delivery pass — its forgetting and orphan sweep included — on every tick, and once a broker resolves registers the activation topic and the `change_notification` type and lets the pass call silence and publish
- A broker that appears later, or returns after a loss, is picked up on a later tick, and delivery catches up from the `awaiting` states; the outage counted no delivery

**Error Scenarios**:
- A `redeliver_interval_seconds` or a `mutation_lock_wait_seconds` of `0` or above its ceiling (43200 and 3600), or an `activation_report_timeout_seconds` below `2 × redeliver_interval_seconds` or above `86400`: gear init refuses the configuration
- No broker resolvable, or the root tenant's id unknown: the tick refreshes the gauges and runs the pass's forgetting and orphan sweep only; writes commit and their states stay `awaiting`, no delivery counted
- The registration refused: logged at `warn` and retried on the next tick; publishes and the call of silence stay off meanwhile, while the forgetting and the orphan sweep run
- The gear's `produce` grant missing: each publish is refused and logged at `warn`, the states stay `awaiting`, and the gear keeps serving
- The broker lost during a pass, or `stop` during one: the pass is abandoned or finished within the shutdown deadline, and every `awaiting` state is kept

**Steps**: steps 11 and 12 (`inst-ca-lifecycle-16`, `inst-ca-lifecycle-17`) are standing rules — for every publish of this feature and for the consumer of entry 2.12 — not actions of this loop's pass. Every publish of this feature runs under the publishing identity of entry 2.12, which holds the `produce` grants this feature adds on the activation topic.

1. [x] - `p1` - At gear init, read `redeliver_interval_seconds`, a whole number of seconds from `1` to `43200` (half a day), default `30` — the ceiling under which a valid `activation_report_timeout_seconds`, at least `2 × redeliver_interval_seconds` and at most `86400`, always exists - `inst-ca-lifecycle-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - Read `mutation_lock_wait_seconds`, a whole number of seconds from `1` to `3600` (one hour), default `10`; a budget above the platform request deadline is accepted and documented as a misconfiguration, since the batch cap by the remainder of the request deadline enforces the deadline - `inst-ca-lifecycle-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** either is `0` or above its ceiling → refuse the configuration and fail init; the shared `publish_timeout_seconds` is read and validated by entry 2.12 (`inst-rc-lifecycle-1`) - `inst-ca-lifecycle-3`
4. [x] - `p1` - Read `activation_report_timeout_seconds`, a whole number of seconds, default `600`; **IF** it is below `2 × redeliver_interval_seconds`, so an instance would get fewer than two deliveries before it is called silent, **OR** above `86400` (one day) → refuse the configuration and fail init - `inst-ca-lifecycle-20`
5. [x] - `p1` - Convert it once to `K = ceil(activation_report_timeout_seconds / redeliver_interval_seconds)` accepted deliveries, `20` at the defaults, which every pass uses (`inst-ca-deliver-21`) - `inst-ca-lifecycle-21`
6. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the gear runs the single-process SQLite profile → at init remove the lock markers a crash may have left, through `remove_lock_marker_at_startup`, for the scan lock and for the mutation lock of every declared key; in every other profile never - `inst-ca-lifecycle-4`
7. [x] - `p1` - Init neither resolves the broker nor waits for it, and the broker is never in the gear's `deps` - `inst-ca-lifecycle-5`
8. [x] - `p1` - On `start`, run the delivery loop on every replica as a `stateful` background task of the gear's lifecycle under the runtime's cancellation token, and log its start at `info` - `inst-ca-lifecycle-6`
9. [x] - `p1` - **WHILE** the cancellation token has not fired, run a pass at start, after a random jitter of at most one second so the replicas do not align, and then once every `redeliver_interval_seconds` — this loop owns the tick, and its retries ride on it - `inst-ca-lifecycle-7`
   1. [x] - `p1` - DB: count the `subscriptions` rows, the `activation_state` rows with status `awaiting`, those with status `silent` and the subscriptions with `silent_since` set, and set `settings_notify_subscriptions`, `settings_activation_awaiting`, `settings_activation_silent` and `settings_activation_silent_subscriptions` from them, whether or not a broker resolves - `inst-ca-lifecycle-8`
   2. [x] - `p1` - Resolve `EventBrokerApi` from the `ClientHub` with a lookup that answers "none" rather than failing; **IF** none → run one pass of `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-deliver` reporting no broker, so only its forgetting and orphan sweep run, and end this tick; the next tick starts after the interval - `inst-ca-lifecycle-9`
   3. [x] - `p1` - Read the root tenant's id from the cache the replica's consumer task fills (`inst-rc-lifecycle-4`); **IF** it is not yet known → run one pass reporting the root tenant's id unknown, so only its forgetting and orphan sweep run, and end this tick; the next tick starts after the interval; the delivery loop never resolves the root tenant itself - `inst-ca-lifecycle-10`
   4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the activation topic and the `change_notification` type are not yet registered → register the topic, and the type as a derived event schema of the broker's event base that names the activation subject type as its allowed subject type and keeps the default partition key `tenant_id`, idempotently - `inst-ca-lifecycle-11`
   5. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the registration is refused or fails → log at `warn`, run one pass reporting the activation types not registered, so only its forgetting and orphan sweep run, and end this tick; the next tick starts after the interval and registers again - `inst-ca-lifecycle-12`
   6. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the registration succeeds → record it, so the publish, the call of silence and the publishing of the scan stop skipping for that reason; a publish refused within the broker's specification-refresh interval after the first registration is retried like any refusal - `inst-ca-lifecycle-19`
   7. [x] - `p1` - Run one pass of `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-deliver` with its broker-dependent part on - `inst-ca-lifecycle-13`
   8. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the pass reports the broker lost → log at `warn`, keep every `awaiting` state, and end this tick; the next tick starts after the interval and resolves the broker again - `inst-ca-lifecycle-14`
10. [x] - `p1` - On `stop`, the token having fired, let the pass in flight finish or abandon it within the runtime's shutdown deadline (`inst-ca-deliver-13`), keeping every `awaiting` state - `inst-ca-lifecycle-15`

## 3. Processes / Business Logic (CDSL)

### Key Mutation Lock

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-algo-consumer-activation-lock`

**Input**: A setting key, the mutation to run under its lock — a value change, a restriction change, a subscription registration or an unsubscribe — and the wait budget: `mutation_lock_wait_seconds`, in a batch further capped by the remainder of the request deadline

**Output**: The mutation's own result with the lock released, or `Unavailable` with nothing of the mutation done

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - Name the lock from the gear name and the setting key, so one key has one lock across every replica and two keys never share one - `inst-ca-lock-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - Take it only after the mutation's authorization and validation passed, so a refused mutation never contends for it - `inst-ca-lock-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - Attempt `try_lock` through `toolkit-db`'s `LockManager`; **WHILE** it is not acquired **AND** the budget lasts → wait on the lock's own retry schedule — a first wait of tens of milliseconds, doubling, capped at one second, with jitter, bounded by the budget and injectable in tests, separate from the 1 s to 30 s backoff of the broker — and attempt again; waiters are not served in order - `inst-ca-lock-3`
4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the budget is exhausted → count `exhausted` on `settings_mutation_lock_total` and observe the wait on `settings_mutation_lock_wait_seconds` - `inst-ca-lock-4`
5. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the budget is exhausted → **RETURN** `Unavailable` with nothing of the mutation done - `inst-ca-lock-11`
6. [x] - `p1` - Count `acquired` on `settings_mutation_lock_total` and observe the wait on `settings_mutation_lock_wait_seconds` - `inst-ca-lock-5`
7. [x] - `p1` - Hold the lock of one key at a time: a mutation never takes a second key's lock while it holds one, so the locks cannot deadlock - `inst-ca-lock-6`
8. [x] - `p1` - Run the mutation under it — a value change from its candidate step until its transaction rolled back, or committed and the local cache is evicted; a restriction change, a registration or an unsubscribe around its transaction - `inst-ca-lock-7`
9. [x] - `p1` - Release the lock and await the release before the mutation returns - `inst-ca-lock-8`
10. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the unlock fails (`NotHeld`, a database error) → log at `warn` with the key, and count `error` on `settings_mutation_lock_total`; a lost lock session is silent to the holder (`toolkit-db` reports it as already released) — the residual DESIGN-activation §6 records - `inst-ca-lock-9`
11. [x] - `p1` - **RETURN** the mutation's result; the guarantee rests on the lock, not on the isolation level, and it holds only while the settings database is reached through session pooling or a direct connection - `inst-ca-lock-10`

### Affected Subscriptions

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-algo-consumer-activation-affected`

**Input**: One change — its key, Scope Class and `secret` trait, the written scope `X`, the operation and its pre-image — the subscriptions qualified under the lock with, for a `cascading` key, each subscription tenant's ancestor chain, and the change's open transaction

**Output**: The affected subscriptions, each with the effective value at its tenant after the change

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - **FOR EACH** qualified subscription of tenant `T` - `inst-ca-affected-1`
   1. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the key is `cascading` **AND** `T` is not `X` → DB: SELECT, in the transaction, the overrides of the key at the tenants of `T`'s chain from `T` up to but excluding `X`; **IF** one exists → exclude the subscription, since a nearer override shadows the change - `inst-ca-affected-2`
   2. [x] - `p1` - Resolve the effective value at `T` after the change through the Value Resolver's in-transaction form — reading through the transaction over `T`'s chain for a `cascading` key, bypassing and never populating the effective-value cache, calling no resolver; a `global` key resolves at the root and a `local` key at `X` - `inst-ca-affected-3`
   3. [x] - `p1` - Take the value before the change at `T` from the change's pre-image when `X` held an override, or from the value `X` inherited when the change creates a new override, read in the same transaction over the same chain - `inst-ca-affected-4`
   4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the key has the `secret` trait → the subscription is affected, since the pre-image holds only a reference and every write counts - `inst-ca-affected-5`
   5. [x] - `p1` - **ELSE IF** the value after the change differs from the value before it in their canonical encoding → the subscription is affected - `inst-ca-affected-6`
   6. [x] - `p1` - **ELSE** → the subscription is not affected and gets no row: a write of an identical value affects nothing - `inst-ca-affected-7`
2. [x] - `p1` - The changes of one request are evaluated in order, each under its own lock and transaction, so the last change to a key at a tenant wins the row - `inst-ca-affected-8`
3. [x] - `p1` - **RETURN** the affected subscriptions with their values after the change - `inst-ca-affected-9`

### Expected Value Encoding

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-algo-consumer-activation-expected`

**Input**: The effective value at a subscription's tenant after the change — for a `secret`-trait key the plaintext the change holds — the key, the subscription's tenant and whether the key has the `secret` trait

**Output**: The `expected_value` text the row stores, and the same encoding the SDK applies to `applied_value`

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the key has no `secret` trait → encode the value as RFC 8785 canonical JSON and **RETURN** those bytes as text, compared later byte for byte - `inst-ca-expected-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - Rely on the number domain the Type Validator enforces on write (`inst-vw-commit-1`, numeric canonicality): every number survives a round trip through IEEE-754 binary64; the encoder never normalizes a number, and one outside that domain fails the change with nothing stored - `inst-ca-expected-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - Encode a `file-reference`-trait value as an ordinary structured value: it carries a version-pinned reference, never the file, and needs no hash - `inst-ca-expected-3`
4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the key has the `secret` trait → build the array of the literal `cf.settings.activation.secret.v1`, the key, the subscription's tenant as a bare lowercase UUID string and the plaintext value, encode it as RFC 8785 canonical JSON, and take its SHA-256 as lowercase hex - `inst-ca-expected-4`
   1. [x] - `p1` - Salt with the key and the tenant only, never with anything of the change, so one value hashes equal across the changes that replace one another on a row - `inst-ca-expected-5`
   2. [x] - `p1` - Take the plaintext from the request for an inline secret `set`, from the fetch for an inherited fallback or an adopted staged entry, or the default's value when the fallback is not a stored secret - `inst-ca-expected-6`
5. [x] - `p1` - The SDK applies this same encoding and hash to `applied_value` in the consumer's process; agreement of the two canonicalizers on a shared corpus is a conformance requirement - `inst-ca-expected-7`
6. [x] - `p1` - **RETURN** the encoded text - `inst-ca-expected-8`

### Outcome Rules

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-algo-consumer-activation-outcome`

**Input**: An `awaiting` or `silent` activation state of the reporting instance — its `change_ref`, `expected_value` and whether its key has the `secret` trait — and the instance's report's `change_ref`, `status`, `applied_value` and `detail`

**Output**: `succeeded`, `failed` or `ignored`, and for a transition the fields it writes

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - Treat `applied_value` as matching only when it is present, well formed — valid RFC 8785 canonical JSON, or 64 lowercase hex characters for a `secret`-trait row — and byte-equal to `expected_value` - `inst-ca-outcome-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - **IF** `status` is `success` **AND** the value matches → **RETURN** `succeeded`, whatever `change_ref` the report names: the consumer runs the value currently expected - `inst-ca-outcome-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** `status` is `success` **AND** the value is different, missing or malformed → **RETURN** `failed` when the report names the row's `change_ref`, `ignored` as stale when it names another - `inst-ca-outcome-3`
4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** `status` is `failed` → **RETURN** `failed` when the report names the row's `change_ref`, `ignored` when it names another - `inst-ca-outcome-4`
5. [x] - `p1` - A transition writes only `status`, `applied_value` as reported (absent when the report carried none), `detail` (the consumer's detail on a `failed` status, absent otherwise), `reported_at` and `updated_at`; `expected_value` and `change_ref` are never touched by a report - `inst-ca-outcome-5`
6. [x] - `p1` - Decide a `silent` state exactly as an `awaiting` one: silence is not terminal, so a late report is accounted as a timely one — a matching `success` settles it `succeeded`, a `failed` or a mismatching `success` naming its `change_ref` settles it `failed` — and the transition keeps its `silent_since`, so the late confirmation stays visible; an `ignored` answer on it leaves it to the re-arm of `inst-ca-report-25` - `inst-ca-outcome-6`

### Notification Bundle

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-algo-consumer-activation-bundle`

**Input**: One pair of subscriber and tenant and its due `awaiting` rows, of any of the subscriber's instances, each with its key and `change_ref`

1. [x] - `p1` - Build one body: `tenant` the pair's tenant (the root tenant's id for a platform-wide subscription), `subscriber` the pair's subscriber, and `changes`, the list of every row's `key` with its `change_ref`, in key order - `inst-ca-bundle-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - Carry identifiers only: no value, no expected value, no hash, no actor, no instance, and no key outside the pair's due `awaiting` rows - `inst-ca-bundle-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - Carry each key once, however many instances of the subscriber hold a due row of it — every row of one subscriber at one key and tenant names the same `change_ref` — so one notification reaches every instance with an interest at the tenant and never one per instance - `inst-ca-bundle-6`
4. [x] - `p1` - Leave the optional `subject_type` and `subject_id` absent in v1 - `inst-ca-bundle-3`

## 4. States (CDSL)

### Activation State Machine

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-state-consumer-activation-row`

The `ActivationStatus` of one instance's state, one per `(subscriber, instance_id, key, tenant)`.

**States**: `awaiting`, `succeeded`, `failed`, `silent`

**Initial State**: `awaiting`, written by the first committed change that expects the subscription

**Transitions**:
1. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** no row **TO** `awaiting` **WHEN** a committed change expects the subscription (`cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-record`) - `inst-ca-row-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `awaiting` **TO** `succeeded` **WHEN** a `success` report of the instance carries the expected value, whatever `change_ref` it names - `inst-ca-row-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `awaiting` **TO** `failed` **WHEN** a report of the instance naming the row's `change_ref` is `failed`, or is a `success` whose value differs, is missing or is malformed - `inst-ca-row-3`
4. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `awaiting` **TO** `awaiting` **WHEN** a later committed change expects the subscription, replacing `expected_value` and `change_ref`, clearing `last_notified_at` and resetting `deliveries` to `0` - `inst-ca-row-4`
5. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `succeeded`, `failed` or `silent` **TO** `awaiting` **WHEN** a later committed change expects the subscription, clearing `applied_value`, `detail`, `reported_at`, `silent_since` and `last_notified_at` and resetting `deliveries` to `0` - `inst-ca-row-5`
6. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** any state **TO** deleted **WHEN** the subscription is unsubscribed — by its handle or by the process's graceful stop — forgotten, evicted by the registration cap or purged with its declaration, or the delivery loop's sweep finds its subscription gone - `inst-ca-row-6`
7. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `awaiting` **TO** `silent` **WHEN** the delivery loop finds the row due with `deliveries` at or above `K` and its guarded update still matches the row's `change_ref` and `awaiting` status, setting `silent_since` - `inst-ca-row-7`
8. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `silent` **TO** `succeeded` **WHEN** a late `success` report of the instance carries the expected value, whatever `change_ref` it names, `silent_since` kept - `inst-ca-row-8`
9. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `silent` **TO** `failed` **WHEN** a late report of the instance naming the row's `change_ref` is `failed`, or is a `success` whose value differs, is missing or is malformed, `silent_since` kept - `inst-ca-row-9`
10. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `silent` **TO** `awaiting` **WHEN** its instance re-enters by a stale report — a stale `change_ref` with another value, or a stale `failed` — or by a registration, re-armed with `deliveries` `0` and `silent_since` and `last_notified_at` cleared, so the row is due at the next scan - `inst-ca-row-10`
11. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** any state **TO** deleted **WHEN** a later committed change leaves the instance out as silent while a sibling of its subscriber at the key and tenant is expected, so no row of the earlier change survives beside the new ones - `inst-ca-row-11`

The transitions without a report are the call of silence (`inst-ca-row-7`), counted in accepted deliveries, not timed; the re-arm by a registration of the instance (`inst-ca-row-10`); a later change, which writes, overwrites or, for an instance it leaves out as silent, deletes a state (`inst-ca-row-1`, `inst-ca-row-4`, `inst-ca-row-5`, `inst-ca-row-11`); and the deletion of a state with its subscription — an unsubscribe, a graceful stop, a forgetting, an eviction, a purge — or by the orphan sweep (`inst-ca-row-6`). No state reaches `succeeded` or `failed` without a report, a report never moves a `succeeded` or `failed` state, and no state is deleted on a timer except with its subscription's forgetting a day after it was first called silent. `silent` is not terminal.

The change outcome of a subscriber at one key and tenant is derived at read time over its instances' states — all naming one `change_ref` — and never stored, in precedence order: `failed` if any state is `failed`, whatever the others are; else `incomplete` if any is `silent`; else `awaiting` if any is `awaiting`; else `succeeded`. `M`, the expected count, is the number of states and `N`, the confirmed count, the number of `succeeded` ones; the derivation runs over the instances still registered, so an unsubscribe, a forgetting or an eviction re-derives it while the history keeps every report and every call of silence. The read that renders it is entry 2.14's; this feature's call of silence judges it only to count `settings_activation_incomplete_total` (`inst-ca-deliver-26`).

### Subscription Key State Machine

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-state-consumer-activation-handle-key`

**States**: `pending` (with a reason, `NotRegistered` among them), `registered`, `refused`

**Initial State**: `pending`, the state of every key of a handle `subscribe` returns, unless the interest limit refused the call

**End States**: `refused` is terminal; a `registered` or `pending` key leaves the handle when `unsubscribe()` answers it `unsubscribed` or `404`, or when the process's `stop` unsubscribes every subscription of the process under its `instance_id` and leaves the groups (`inst-ca-unsubscribe-19`, `inst-ca-unsubscribe-12`), which ends every handle in the process — gone from the handle, which is not a state, and neither is unsubscribed

**Transitions**:
1. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `pending` **TO** `registered` **WHEN** the key's registration committed and its fresh read returned a value or `Retired` - `inst-ca-hk-1`
2. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `pending` **TO** `pending` **WHEN** a retryable outcome repeats; the SDK retries with backoff and logs once each minute - `inst-ca-hk-2`
3. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `pending` **TO** `refused` **WHEN** the registration read decision is a `403` - `inst-ca-hk-3`
4. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `registered` **TO** `pending` **WHEN** the broker refuses the gear's group or join for a missing `define` or `consume` grant, the subscription kept - `inst-ca-hk-4`
5. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `pending` **TO** `registered` **WHEN** the join retried after a missing `define` or `consume` grant succeeds, the subscription kept - `inst-ca-hk-5`
6. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `registered` **TO** gone from the handle **WHEN** `unsubscribe()` released the key — its subscription deleted, or left to another live handle of the gear in this process - `inst-ca-hk-6`
7. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `pending` **TO** gone from the handle **WHEN** `unsubscribe()` released the key or answered `404` for it - `inst-ca-hk-7`
8. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** `registered` **TO** `pending` (`NotRegistered`) **WHEN** `report_outcome` answered `not_subscribed` for the key while a live handle of the gear in this process holds it; the retry registers it again and moves it back to `registered` as any pending registration that commits - `inst-ca-hk-8`

A `Subscription` has no state machine: it exists, or it does not, and while it exists its `silent_since` is set or absent. Dropping a handle ends its keys' states in the process without touching any subscription. The answers of `unsubscribe()` and `report_outcome` belong to their flows, `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-unsubscribe` and `cpt-cf-settings-service-flow-consumer-activation-report`.

### Subscriptions

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-subscriptions`

The Subscription Manager **MUST** register a subscription per subscriber, consumer instance, exact key and tenant, idempotent on `uq_sub_subscriber_instance_key_tenant`, with the tenant normalised to a bare lowercase UUID string, the subscriber taken from the caller's security context, the `instance_id` and the `instance_label`, truncated to 128 characters, taken from the call, and a call without an `instance_id` refused with the typed invalid-input error. ### Every Mutation of a Key Takes Its Lock

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-mutation-lock`

Every value change (`set`, `revert`, `remove_value`, `clone`, each change of a batch), every restriction change (`set_restriction`, `clear_restriction`) and every subscription registration and unsubscribe **MUST** take the key's mutation lock — a named advisory lock through `toolkit-db`'s `LockManager`, keyed on the gear and the setting key — after its authorization and validation, waiting through `try_lock` on the lock's own retry schedule — a first wait of tens of milliseconds, doubling, capped at one second, with jitter — within `mutation_lock_wait_seconds` (default `10`, refused at init when `0` or above `3600`), in a batch further capped by the remainder of the request deadline, the schedule and the budget injectable in tests. An exhausted wait **MUST** answer `Unavailable` with nothing stored — `503` on the single-value and restriction routes, per item in `SetResult[]`, with the batch's later changes still attempted — and **MUST** leave the lock to its owner. A value change **MUST** hold the lock from before its candidate step until its transaction rolled back, or committed and the local cache is evicted, then release it and await the release before answering, so the next mutation of the key acquires it without waiting after a rollback; a restriction change, a registration — its cap eviction and its re-arm included — and a retire **MUST** hold it around their transactions. Declaration lifecycle changes **MUST NOT** take it, nor **MUST** a report, the delivery loop's call of silence or its forgetting, each guarded instead on the row or subscription it changes; and no mutation **MUST** hold two keys' locks at once. Acquisitions **MUST** be counted `acquired`, `exhausted` or `error` and their waits observed; a failed unlock (`NotHeld`, a database error) **MUST** be logged at `warn` and counted `error`; a lost lock session is silent to the holder (`toolkit-db` reports it as already released) — the residual DESIGN-activation §6 records. In the single-process SQLite profile the gear **MUST** remove the lock markers at init for the scan lock and every declared key's lock, and in no other profile. The settings database **MUST** be reached through session pooling or a direct connection; a transaction-pooling proxy voids the lock and is not supported.

### Activation State in the Change's Transaction

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-record`

### Notification Delivery

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-delivery`

At the end of every request that wrote activation states, after its `cache_invalidate` broadcasts, the end-of-request hook **MUST** publish, per pair of subscriber and tenant it touched, the keys of the pair's due `awaiting` states of any of its instances, each key once with its `change_ref`, as `change_notification` on the activation topic, with envelope `tenant_id` and `subject` the subscription's tenant, `subject_type` the activation subject type, `source` `settings-service` and `trace_parent` from the current span (left out when it is not a well-formed W3C value, which the broker would refuse); a bundle over 60 KiB **MUST** be split with keys kept whole; each publish **MUST** be bounded by `publish_timeout_seconds`, the request's remaining publishes skipped after the first refusal or timeout of either signal, and an accepted publish **MUST**, in one guarded UPDATE, stamp `last_notified_at` and count one delivery in `deliveries` on the due rows it bundled that still name the bundled `change_ref`, and on no other row, its row locks taken in the order of `uq_activation_state` (`cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-record`); a refused or timed-out publish **MUST** count none. Without a broker, before the activation types are registered or before the root tenant's id is known, nothing **MUST** be published and the write **MUST** commit. Every replica **MUST** run the delivery loop as one control loop ticking every `redeliver_interval_seconds` (default `30`, refused at init when `0` or above `43200`, the ceiling under which a valid `activation_report_timeout_seconds` — at least `2 × redeliver_interval_seconds` and at most `86400` — always exists), its first pass after a random jitter of at most one second: each tick refreshes the activation gauges and runs one pass whether or not a broker resolves — one non-blocking attempt on the scan lock, the pass skipped when another replica holds it, then the forgetting and an orphan sweep in one conditional delete — and on a tick on which the broker resolves, the root tenant's id is known and the activation types are registered the pass goes on to the call of silence (`cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-report-timeout`) and a re-publish of the due `awaiting` rows only — a row due once it is 0.9 × `redeliver_interval_seconds` old for a tick, the exact interval for the end-of-request publish, so a call of silence can come up to a tenth of an interval earlier than `K × redeliver_interval_seconds` — a silent row never re-published until re-armed, with a stamp and a delivery count guarded by the row's `change_ref`, the rest of the pass abandoned when the broker is lost, and on `stop` the pass finished or abandoned within the shutdown deadline with every `awaiting` state kept. The activation topic and the `change_notification` type **MUST** be registered by the delivery loop's own task once the broker resolves, idempotently, and retried on the next tick after a refusal. The deployment **MUST** grant the publishing identity of entry 2.12 `produce` on the activation topic, the `change_notification` type and the activation subject type for events of every tenant; a missing grant **MUST** leave the gear up and the states `awaiting`, each refused publish logged at `warn` and retried by a later pass.

### Outcome Recording

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-outcomes`

The Activation Outcome Recorder's `receive_response` **MUST** take the subscriber from the security context and the `instance_id` from the call, refuse a call without one with the typed invalid-input error, and decide against the reporting instance's own state, never a sibling's. It **MUST** answer `not_subscribed`, writing nothing, when the instance holds no subscription for the key at the tenant; `ignored` for a subscribed instance with no state of the current change and for a `succeeded` or `failed` state; and, for an `awaiting` or a `silent` state alike, settle `succeeded` a `success` whose value matches the expected value whatever `change_ref` it names, settle `failed` a `success` with a different, missing or malformed value or a `failed` status when the report names the state's `change_ref`, and answer `ignored` for either when it names another. A transition **MUST** write only `status`, `applied_value`, `detail`, `reported_at` and `updated_at`, keeping a `silent` state's `silent_since`, guarded on the state still naming the decided `change_ref` and still `awaiting` or `silent`, a guard matching no row answering `ignored` with no audit record; it **MUST** append, in the same transaction, exactly one `activation` audit record with the key's canonical resource, `declaration_key`, the state's tenant, the subscriber as `actor` with `actor_classification` `public`, the reporting instance's `instance_id` and its subscription's `instance_label`, `outcome`, the consumer's `detail` truncated to 1 KiB on failure, a minted `request_id`, `change_ref` and no pre-value or post-value. A stale report **MUST** re-arm the instance's `silent` state of the current change to `awaiting` with `deliveries` `0` and `silent_since` and `last_notified_at` cleared, guarded on `silent` and the decided `change_ref`, writing no record, while a repeat, decided on a settled state, re-arms nothing; every report but a `not_subscribed` one **MUST** clear the subscription's `silent_since` as the last statement of its transaction, after its row statements, guarded on the instance having no `silent` state for the key and tenant at that moment. A failed append or commit **MUST** roll back and answer an error; a report **MUST** be idempotent per subscriber, instance, key, tenant and `change_ref`, so a repeated report reaches the same end state as the first; late reports **MUST** be accepted while the state is `awaiting` or `silent`. `settings_notify_reports_total` **MUST** count each answer under exactly one of `applied`, `not_subscribed`, `ignored_no_row`, `ignored_stale` and `ignored_repeat` — `ignored_stale` for a decision taken on an expectation no longer current: an earlier `change_ref` not accounted, a re-arm included, or a guarded update that matched nothing; `ignored_repeat` for a state already settled when read (DESIGN-activation §7 *Feature Metrics*).

### Report Timeout, Silence and Forgetting

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-report-timeout`

The system **MUST** read `activation_report_timeout_seconds` (default `600`) at gear init, refuse it below `2 × redeliver_interval_seconds` or above `86400`, and convert it to `K = ceil(activation_report_timeout_seconds / redeliver_interval_seconds)` accepted deliveries. A delivery **MUST** count only as a publication of a state's current `change_ref` that the broker accepted while the state was due — the end-of-request publish and each re-publication of the delivery loop, never a refused or timed-out one and never one that carried the key only for a sibling's due state — so a broker outage counts nothing and cannot make an instance silent. A pass with a broker **MUST**, before it publishes, call silent each due `awaiting` state whose `deliveries` has reached `K`, in one transaction per state and in this order: the state to `silent` with `silent_since` now, guarded on its `change_ref` and `awaiting`; only when that matched, the subscription's `silent_since` unless already set, and one `activation` audit record with `outcome` `silent`, the instance's `instance_id` and `instance_label`, the state's `change_ref`, a minted `request_id` and no `detail`; counting, after the commit, `settings_activation_silenced_total`, and `settings_activation_incomplete_total` when the subscriber's other states of the key and tenant were neither `silent` nor `failed` before the call, judged in the call's transaction. A `silent` state **MUST NOT** be re-published until a re-entry of its instance re-arms it, **MUST NOT** be counted failed, and **MUST** stay settleable by a late report. Every pass, on every tick whether or not a broker resolves, **MUST** take the scan lock and, before its broker-dependent part, forget each subscription whose `silent_since` is older than `SILENT_FORGET_SECONDS` (`86400`, a constant) — its states, then the subscription, in one transaction per subscription, each statement conditional on that `silent_since`, counting `settings_activation_forgotten_total` and writing or altering no audit record — so a subscription a report or a registration cleared meanwhile is kept, and the day is wall time from the first call of silence, neither paused nor extended by an outage. The delivery loop's call of silence and its forgetting **MUST NOT** take a key's mutation lock.

### Consumer Activation SDK

- [x] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-cf-settings-service-dod-consumer-activation-sdk`
